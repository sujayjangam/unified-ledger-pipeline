> **Status: planned 2026-11-19. PR 1 merged 2026-11-19 (#140, migration 0011 on production). PR 2 merged 2026-11-19 (#141). PR 3 built 2026-11-19, with PR 4's change buttons folded in.** Builds

> fictional: Tans (Alice, Bob), Lees (Carol, Dave), Ngs (Erin, Frank). Sender is Alice.

## Goal

"Settle Carol paid 381 for saree pleating and for Erin airbnb" becomes one payback, linked to
the bills it clears, after one Confirm. Python reads it; the model is never called. The screens
are the ones in ADR-0052 and are not redesigned here.

## Decided while planning (agreed 2026-11-19)

ADR-0052 leaves these open. Each is small enough for this plan; none changes a screen.
Paying back in another currency at a known or recent rate is a separate, later issue.

1. **One currency per settle message.** With no currency said, the bills' currency is used. If
   the bills in scope, or the giver's debts to the receiver, are in two currencies, the bot
   refuses: "Carol owes Alice in AUD and SGD. Say which, e.g. 'Settle Carol paid 50 AUD'."
2. **Another currency is converted at a looked-up rate, as a proxy**
   ([#138](https://github.com/sujayjangam/unified-ledger-pipeline/issues/138), in PR 3). On a
   trip the bills are in the local currency and paybacks are in SGD, so this is the usual
   case. "Settle Erin paid 100 SGD for diwali deco" on an INR bill works out what the SGD
   clears from a daily reference rate, shows the rate and its date, warns that the payback is
   in a different currency, and Confirm reads "⚠️ Confirm in SGD". The payback is saved with
   the rate, its date and source, and a "check against statement" flag. A failed lookup never
   blocks: the sender replies with what it clears, as "Mark paid" does. This reverses
   ADR-0046's rejection of market rates, so it is recorded in ADR-0053 (accepted 2026-11-19).
3. **Bill shares are shown gross.** If Bob owes Alice 100 for the airbnb and Alice owes Bob 50
   for the car, `/trip_bals` shows Bob owing 50, but the settle card shows Bob's airbnb share
   as 100. Netting at bill level is what the offset rows in ADR-0052 (with #129) are for.
   Until then, the overpayment check compares against the shares named, as the ADR says.
4. **The people named decide the bills, never the sender.** Candidates are bills where the
   people the money is for (the giver, with no "for") still owe a share. The sender is only
   recorded as who logged it. "Settle Dave paid 1000 for airbnb for the Ngs", sent by Alice,
   matches the airbnb Carol paid; Dave pays Carol. With trip mode off, the candidates are
   untagged bills and bills on any trip, whoever sent the message.
5. **"Gift" wording goes now**, on the old payback card and in `/trip_bals`' "Already paid
   back" lines: "paid on Erin's behalf". #134's done-when asks for it, and it needs no new
   screen.
6. **A receiver may be named.** "Settle Carol paid Alice 381" (also "paid to Alice") is
   Carol paying Alice: the candidates are only bills Alice paid where Carol (or the people
   named after "for") owes, and the "who got it?" buttons are skipped. This only narrows
   ADR-0052's rule that the receiver is whoever paid the bills. A bill named that someone else
   paid is refused: "The airbnb was paid by Bob, not Alice." Owing that person nothing is
   refused too: "Carol owes Alice nothing on the Beach trip."

## Design

**Migration 0011** (hand-written, additive): `settlement_bills`
- `settlement_id` → `settlements.id`, `transaction_id` → `raw_transactions.transaction_id`
  (TEXT), `for_participant_id` → `participants.id`, `cents BIGINT NOT NULL`, `> 0`
- primary key (`settlement_id`, `transaction_id`, `for_participant_id`); index on
  `transaction_id` for "is this bill paid off"
- no `ON DELETE` on any key (ADR-0052)
- on `settlements`, all nullable: the rate used, its date, its source, and a "check against
  statement" flag (decision 2; exact columns in ADR-0053). Old rows keep NULL.

**Writing.** `add_settlement` takes an optional `bills` list and writes it in the same database
transaction as the settlement, only when the settlement row was really inserted, as
`add_expense` does with splits. The rows plus any overpayment must add up to `clears_cents`.
A payback with bill rows has `for_participant_id` NULL: the rows say whose shares it pays.

**Reading.** `get_trip_settlements` gains each payback's bill rows. New reads for the everyday
scope: untagged split entries, untagged paybacks with their rows, and split entries on the
sender's trips. Each returns None on failure, never an empty list.

**Maths (`app/services/balances.py`, pure).**
- `_pay_back` with bill rows: each row takes cents off "for person owes the bill's payer". A
  row on a bill that isn't live (soft-deleted) clears nothing. Whatever a row can't clear, and
  any overpayment, is owed back to the giver, as now. Paybacks without rows run as today.
- `open_bill_shares(entries, settlements)`: what is still owed on each (bill, person), owed to
  the bill's payer. Rows come off their own share. An old payback with no rows clears the
  people `_pay_back` picks today, each on their oldest bills first. `_pay_back` is refactored to
  return who it cleared, so both use one ordering.

**Reader (`app/services/settle.py`, new, pure, no model).**
- `is_settle(text)`: first word "settle", any case, punctuation after it ignored.
- `read_settle(text, people, sender)`: the giver ("I"/"me" is the sender), an optional
  receiver right after "paid" (decision 6), the amount through
  `dollars_to_cents`, an optional currency, and the "for" parts, each split into names (and
  family labels) and bill words. Names use `match_person` and the trip's family labels, as a
  split does. Anything unreadable is a refusal with ADR-0052's wording.
- `match_bills(words, candidates, money_left)`: full, close or no match, exactly as the ADR's
  rules and table. Stop words, hyphens, case and a trailing "s" are dropped; whole words only.
- `allocate(parts, amount, open_shares)`: the rows in the ADR's order (named bills in the order
  said, oldest first inside a part and with no bill named), plus any overpayment.
- `build_settle_card(reading, snapshot, choices)`: one pure function that turns the reading,
  the snapshot and the taps so far (receiver, a "Did you mean" pick, "Pay oldest first", bills
  ticked, everyday switch, giver) into card lines, buttons and rows. Every tap reruns it from
  the reading, never from the last card, as `change_payer` starts from the message (ADR-0038).

**Bot (`app/bot_core.py`).**
- The settle check goes at the top of `process_expense_text`, before the model call, so typed
  and voice settle messages take the same path (CLAUDE.md: inference logic lives there).
- The message's scope is read once: the sender's active trip (fresh, as for an expense), that
  trip's bills and paybacks, and the everyday ones. All of it is kept on the pending card, so
  no tap reads the database or the directory again.
- Its own pending map and callback prefix (`su:`), so it can't clash with the old `settle:`
  cards. Card lock, answer once, toast only on resolved cards, redraw from a copy. Buttons
  carry an index into the snapshot, never a name, under 64 bytes.
- Confirm swaps in "⏳ Saving..." before the save; a failed save puts the card back with its
  idempotency key.

**`/help`.** A "🤝 Settling up" topic with ADR-0052's wordings. `tests/test_help.py` checks
that each settle example is read by `read_settle` in a unit test, instead of looking for it in
`scripts/check_extraction.py`.

## Risks to test

- A settle message that reaches the model and is saved as an expense, counting money twice.
  Tested: a settle message never calls extraction, typed or after transcription.
- Old paybacks: `/trip_bals` must not change for a trip with no bill rows. Tested on a copy of
  the shape of the real trip's rows on the Neon branch.
- Whisper writing "three hundred and eighty one" instead of 381. Digits only in PR 3; a voice
  test on the test bot shows how often it happens.
- A common word ("dinner") fully matching several bills. The ⚠️ "2 bills matched" line and
  Change bills (built in PR 3) is the fix.
- Ten people's shares on one card staying readable on a phone.
- A slow rate lookup making the settle card feel dead. Timed on the test bot (local; no staging Cloud Run exists)
  for first and later lookups of the day before PR 3 merges, then on production right after
  the deploy with a cancelled card (ADR-0053, #137).

## Out of scope

Converting bills or balances to another currency, settling with someone outside the trip or the household, editing or deleting a
saved payback (a `/recent` follow-up), and simplifying debts (#129).
